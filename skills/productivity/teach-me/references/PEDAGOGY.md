# Pedagogy

## Lesson shape

Teach one capability tied to the topic mission and just above the learner's demonstrated floor. Use only the beats that help:

1. establish the useful question;
2. ask for a prediction or conjecture;
3. reveal the mechanism one meaningful change at a time;
4. give immediate authored feedback;
5. test transfer on a different example;
6. expose the strongest frozen sources.

Keep prose subordinate to the mechanism. A difficult transfer or synthesis task is better evidence than repeating the demonstration.

## Visual systems

Use progressive ERDs, architecture boundaries, request/data flows, ownership movement, and state transitions when relationships carry the explanation. Begin with the minimum structure and mute irrelevant parts. Let stable semantic IDs drive animation.

## Code

Authored traces need not execute. Make the active line and before/after phase explicit. Separate source, variables, literal console output, conceptual call state, and data movement. Use three to five representative cases—normally typical, high-volume/many-data, empty/no-input, and one meaningful edge case—without cataloguing niche curiosities.

## Mathematics

For real-valued math, begin with a graph or geometric question. Let the learner explore a parameter and form a conjecture before the formula. Connect symbolic steps to visible geometric change; include a counterexample or boundary that breaks the easy guess.

## Fidelity and records

- `exact`: matches the defined program, source, data, or mathematics.
- `simplified`: omits irrelevant detail while preserving shown relationships.
- `conceptual`: a teaching model, not a claim about literal internals.

Put the label beside the relevant state. Record learning only after evidence; do not equate exposure, completion, or quick recognition with durable understanding.
